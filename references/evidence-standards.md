# Evidence Standards

Evidence quality determines whether a candidate can pass gates. Low-quality evidence can help measure narrative spread or crowding, but cannot anchor an S-level thesis.

## High-Grade Evidence

High-grade evidence may support the core S-level thesis:

- Company announcements and exchange filings.
- Annual, quarterly, and interim financial reports.
- Earnings calls, investor day transcripts, and management Q&A.
- Official order announcements, supply agreements, customer qualification, or design wins.
- Industry price data, inventory data, utilization, lead times, capacity allocation, and credible third-party supply-chain checks.
- Official policy documents, tariff decisions, export-control changes, regulatory approvals, and government procurement budgets.
- Overseas leader mapping: guidance, filings, earnings calls, price action, capex plans, and order comments from global leaders that directly map to the theme.
- Verified product releases, benchmark disclosures, architecture changes, or standard-setting events.

## Medium-Grade Evidence

Medium-grade evidence may support context, but should be corroborated before S:

- Reputable financial media.
- Sell-side industry research with traceable assumptions.
- Conference notes from identifiable institutions.
- Alternative data with methodology disclosed.
- Market data such as price, volume, breadth, relative strength, options, and cross-asset confirmation.

## Low-Grade Evidence

Low-grade evidence cannot be used as the core basis for S:

- 雪球 posts.
- 股吧 posts.
- 微博 posts.
- 微信群 or Telegram screenshots.
- Anonymous 小作文.
- Unverifiable supply-chain rumors.
- Influencer threads without primary sources.
- Forum discussion heat.

Use low-grade evidence only to judge:

- Whether a narrative is spreading.
- Whether retail crowding is rising.
- Whether a theme is becoming noisy.
- Which claims require verification.

## Evidence Table Requirements

Every S report must include a table with:

- Claim.
- Evidence source.
- Evidence grade.
- Date.
- Link or citation when available.
- Which gate it supports.
- Remaining uncertainty.

## Evidence Failure Rules

- If the core catalyst has no high-grade evidence, the candidate cannot be S.
- If the only bullish evidence is price action, the candidate cannot be S.
- If the evidence cannot map to revenue, margin, supply-demand, valuation, or risk-discount change, the candidate cannot be S.
- If low-grade evidence conflicts with high-grade evidence, prioritize high-grade evidence and use low-grade evidence only as crowding context.
