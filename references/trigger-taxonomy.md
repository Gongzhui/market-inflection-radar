# Trigger Taxonomy

Use this taxonomy to classify the source of the expectation-curve change.

## 1. Capability Shock

A new product, model, architecture, or benchmark changes demand assumptions.

Examples:

- Frontier AI model capability jump.
- New accelerator architecture increasing memory, networking, cooling, or power requirements.
- Breakthrough manufacturing yield or process capability.

S-level requirement: the capability shock must map to quantifiable demand or supply constraints, not only broad excitement.

## 2. Supply-Demand Tightening

Pricing, inventory, utilization, lead time, capacity, or order data shows a tightening cycle.

Examples:

- DRAM/HBM supply allocation.
- Optical module capacity tightness.
- Foundry or advanced packaging utilization turning.

S-level requirement: data must imply a multi-quarter earnings revision path for high-purity assets.

## 3. Risk-Discount Removal

A severe political, regulatory, tariff, sanction, or geopolitical risk discount is partially removed.

Examples:

- Trade-war risk easing for core AI supply chain assets.
- Export-control fear proving less destructive than priced.
- Regulatory overhang resolving.

S-level requirement: the asset must be a core beneficiary whose fundamental demand curve was already strong before the discount removal.

## 4. Policy With Earnings Transmission

Policy can be a catalyst only if it creates measurable orders, subsidies, pricing power, market access, or capex visibility.

S-level requirement: policy language alone is insufficient. There must be a transmission path to revenue, margin, or terminal value.

## 5. Overseas Leader Mapping

A global leader's price action, guidance, order comments, or capex plan reveals a change in an industry curve that maps to domestic or adjacent assets.

Examples:

- Micron guidance or price action confirming memory tightness.
- NVIDIA or hyperscaler capex signals confirming AI infrastructure demand.

S-level requirement: mapping must be explicit. Do not infer domestic beneficiaries merely because they share a theme label.

## 6. New Order or Customer Validation

Large orders, design wins, supply agreements, or customer qualification can trigger an S-level scan.

S-level requirement: customer validation must be material relative to the company's baseline and connected to a large industry curve.
